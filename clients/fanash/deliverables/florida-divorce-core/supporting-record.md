## Scope and decision

**Recommendation:** Replace the current informational-law framing with a Florida divorce service hub that helps a prospective client understand eligibility, process choices, core decisions, and Fanash's role. Retain the existing implementation URL. No redirect, migration, canonical change, publication, or live-site edit is proposed.

**Business significance:** The current page mixes broad legal education with a lawyer-intent title, uses an unverified consultation claim, and sends readers to destinations that do not follow the governing V2 hub relationships. The proposed page aligns the search promise, client decision, verified services, and consultation path without forecasting rankings or leads.

**Bounded research:** Inspection was limited to the current hub, Fanash first-party pages needed to establish identity, voice, contact details, service availability, and V2 destinations, plus current Florida primary authority and the official Florida Bar profile. No broad crawl, competitor research, analytics, keyword platform, backlink analysis, form submission, intake call, or performance estimate was used.

**Completion condition:** One complete DOCX, one supporting record, one independent read-only `seo_reviewer` pass, reconciliation of material findings, deterministic validation, final render inspection, hyperlink checks, mechanical QA, and current legal-accuracy QA.

## Observation, hypothesis, recommendation, and test

- **Observation:** The live page returned `200` with zero redirects, is indexable and self-canonical, and used the H1 `Florida Divorce Laws`. Its body contained about 1,310 page-content words, below V2's 1,800–2,600-word target.
- **Observation:** The title tag targeted `Divorce Lawyer in Florida`, while the opening and H1 emphasized general legal education. The meta description also advertised free consultations without corroboration on the scoped contact material.
- **Observation:** Some current hub links point to alimony, custody, child-support, and relocation hubs or branches without a direct outgoing V2 relationship from `FL-PA-DIV`.
- **Hypothesis:** Better alignment between lawyer intent, early service clarity, and direct child routing should reduce ambiguity for prospective clients. No conversion or ranking result can be inferred without measurement.
- **Recommendation:** Publish only after attorney/compliance review, destination repairs noted below, and implementation QA. Retain the URL and use the proposed metadata and copy as the implementation source.
- **Test:** After publication, annotate the change date and compare qualified divorce inquiries, organic landing-page sessions, engagement, and conversions against a seasonally reasonable baseline. Treat rankings and clicks as diagnostics, not final outcomes.

### Architecture and client-offer conflicts

1. **Legal Separation:** V2 treats `FL-M006` as a Required Core Procedure with a canonical path. Florida does not provide a formal legal-separation marital status. Fanash can offer related support and parenting remedies, and its existing page correctly explains that distinction. The hub links only as `Florida alternatives to legal separation`; it does not present an unavailable filing status.
2. **Collaborative Divorce:** V2 treats `FL-M003` as Required and canonical. Fanash has no reliable live destination or sufficient scoped proof that the firm offers collaborative representation. The required branch is documented but excluded.
3. **High-Conflict Divorce:** V2 treats `FL-M008` as Required. Fanash has a technically live destination, but its cross-topic metadata and copy make it unsafe to recommend as a current internal destination. The hub discusses heightened planning without linking that page.
4. **Military Divorce:** V2 requires service and local-demand validation. The service half is supported, while standalone local-demand evidence is outside this bounded task. The link is excluded without inventing gate approval.
5. **Current cross-branch links:** Alimony, child custody, child support, and relocation are important divorce issues, but V2 does not provide a direct outgoing relationship from `FL-PA-DIV` to those destinations. The proposal explains the issues without adding unsupported hub-to-hub or custody-child links.

## Reusable lesson candidate

**Demonstrated pattern:** A V2 child can be `Required` and `PASS — CANONICAL` while a jurisdiction makes its generic service label unavailable, or while the client's corresponding destination remains unverified or publication-unsafe. Requiredness is architecture evidence, not service, jurisdiction, or destination clearance.

Proposed narrow workflow convention: for every excluded Required direct child, record the V2 status, client/jurisdiction evidence, exclusion reason, and reactivation condition. Do not silently relabel the node, invent a URL, or treat a technically live page as publishable evidence.

Frozen checks for reviewer assessment:

1. **Original scenario:** `FL-M006` Legal Separation in Florida. Record the conflict and use only a qualified alternatives framing supported by current law and the client destination.
2. **Different relevant scenario:** `FL-M008` High-Conflict Divorce. A `200` destination with cross-topic metadata and body defects remains excluded until repaired.
3. **Unaffected scenario:** `FL-M004` Contested Divorce. A direct, published, jurisdiction-matched, clean service destination remains eligible and is linked once.

No persistent workflow file will be changed until the independent reviewer assesses whether this convention is genuinely new, scoped, and safe.
