# Evidence-based editorial rubric (pilot candidate v1)

Adapted 2026-09-23 from the comparison release's `editorial-rubric.md` for use inside the
content-workflow pilot. Use it with the approved brief, the routed voice source, the approved
client facts, the architecture, and the current draft. Examples are fictional.

## Observable criteria

| Dimension | Evidence to inspect | Pass evidence | Material defect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Client identity | Routed domain, the one voice source, current approved style direction | Naming, jurisdiction, and voice derive from the routed client | Wrong client, mixed voices, a voice chosen by name pattern |
| Scenario relevance | The reader's problem, case stage, next decision | Opening and body address that problem and make the next decision clearer | Broad hub copy displaces the situation; a material scenario constraint is ignored |
| Explanatory angle | Documented audience priorities in the voice source | Concepts are connected to what this audience must understand without changing the law | A documented priority is contradicted where it matters; a slogan substitutes for explanation |
| Client evidence | Approved client facts with scope and dates | Offers, fees, process, credentials, staffing, coverage, and differentiators stay within supported scope | Unsupported offer, invented proof, a limited service generalized to every matter |
| Structure and plain language | Purpose, sequence, headings, qualifications | The page answers clearly, keeps material conditions, links deeper process | Hub content overwhelms the scenario; a missing condition misleads; organization hides the next step |
| Trust and sensitivity | Reader state, actual urgency, contact process, safety context | Calm, specific, proportionate guidance and a truthful next action | Manufactured urgency, guaranteed outcome, fear amplification, unsupported consultation promise |
| Links and citations | Purpose, link plan, destination, source register | Each placement serves understanding, navigation, contact, or evidence; source identity is stable | Wrong destination, misleading anchor, invented URL, a second number for the same source |

Do not compute a uniqueness percentage or require branded language in every paragraph. A neutral
statement of the same law may be correct for many firms. Assess whether the complete page helps the
specified reader and whether client-specific choices are useful, accurate, and evidenced.

## Client specificity decisions

| Evidence and draft | Sound decision | Unsound decision |
|---|---|---|
| Calm practical voice approved; draft explains verified legal options neutrally and connects them to the reader's scheduling concern | Accept the neutral explanation and the scenario connection | Fail because another firm's paragraph could sound similar |
| Brief centers a parent's schedule uncertainty; draft opens with generic divorce history and never reaches the scheduling decision | Flag the displaced intent with exact passages and the brief criterion | Say only "the middle is generic" or demand more firm-name mentions |
| A fixed fee is approved for one service; draft promises fixed fees for every contested matter | Flag the scope overstatement (`client-fact` or `promise`) with the approved-fact scope | Accept because the voice source mentions fixed fees somewhere |
| No consultation-price fact is approved; CTA says "free consultation" | Open a `promise` finding; propose a verified neutral contact invitation | Infer "free" from a competitor or from style approval |
| A verified statutory qualification would be removed by a suggested brand rewrite | Preserve the qualification; route accuracy questions to the legal reviewer | Make the rule sound distinctive at the cost of its meaning |

## Closure

A finding stays `open` until the coordinator applies a change and you re-read the revised passage;
copy it into `resolution.corrected_text` for `client-fact`, `promise`, and `citation` findings.
A proposed fix, a change log, or a coordinator note never closes a finding; the coordinator cannot
accept a client-fact or promise error as a risk.
