# Family Law Paid Landing Page Strategist

## Purpose

Use this Skill to plan or audit paid landing pages for family law firms. The goal is not generic CRO. The goal is emotionally credible, trust-heavy, action-oriented pages that help the right prospect take the next step.

## Family Law Operating Reality

Treat family law marketing as a high-emotion, high-urgency, high-risk category. The work is not generic legal marketing and not generic SEO.

Core domain assumptions that must shape all decisions:
- Search intent is often a snapshot of a person in crisis, conflict, uncertainty, or protection mode. Queries frequently represent emotional and procedural states in a timeline, not just keywords.
- Trust beats cleverness. Proof, specificity, jurisdictional fluency, attorney credibility, and clear next steps usually matter more than abstract brand language.
- Local relevance is decisive. County, city, courthouse, and jurisdiction nuances can materially change what content should exist, how it should be framed, and what converts.
- Unsupported superiority claims hurt credibility. Avoid "best," "top," "guaranteed," or specialist/certification language unless the source material proves it.
- Do not invent legal rules, process details, outcomes, or firm differentiators. Separate evidence, inference, and unknowns.
- Conversion psychology is emotional and protective, not merely rational. Fear, urgency, confusion, concern for children, concern for assets, and anxiety about court all matter.

## Conversion assumptions that must guide this Skill

- Family law prospects often arrive anxious, flooded, ashamed, angry, overwhelmed, or urgently protective. Pages should reduce confusion without sounding manipulative.
- Trust and clarity usually matter more than clever hooks.
- Different family law matters require different emotional sequencing. Divorce, emergency custody, protective situations, modifications, mediation, and asset disputes should not sound interchangeable.
- Paid pages must quickly answer: "Am I in the right place?", "Can these attorneys handle my problem?", and "What happens next?"
- Pages should avoid a SaaS tone, macho sales tone, or vague luxury tone unless the firm's evidence and market truly support it.
- Form friction and call-to-action design should reflect urgency and sensitivity.

## Use this Skill when

- creating or auditing PPC landing pages for family law matters
- aligning ad intent to page structure and conversion flow
- improving consultation conversion on service-specific pages
- adjusting messaging for different emotional states or legal scenarios

## Do not use this Skill when

- the task is broad site architecture or content prioritization
- the task is pure brand strategy
- the task is long-form SEO informational content
- the task is a final executive report

## Inputs to review

- ad themes or keywords
- target service or scenario
- target geography
- current landing page or wireframe
- intake goals and CTA options
- trust assets: reviews, bios, awards, consultation process, outcomes language, FAQs
- known objections from intake or sales calls

## Workflow

1. **Identify the exact intent**
   - Clarify whether the page is targeting hire-now intent, urgent protection intent, process-explanation intent, or comparison-shopping intent.
   - Clarify the service and geography.

2. **Map the emotional state**
   - Identify likely fears, blockers, and practical questions.
   - Adjust the page sequence accordingly.

3. **Define the page promise**
   - The opening should quickly establish relevance, seriousness, and next-step clarity.
   - Avoid guarantees or exaggerated certainty.

4. **Build the trust stack**
   - Decide what proof should appear and in what order.
   - Prefer specific experience, attorney credibility, process clarity, and locally relevant proof over vague badges.

5. **Sequence the page**
   A strong family law paid page often includes some version of:
   - headline and relevance confirmation
   - empathetic but controlled framing
   - service/problem clarity
   - why this firm
   - trust evidence
   - process or what-to-expect
   - objections / FAQs
   - CTA reinforcement
   - secondary trust and CTA close

6. **Design the CTA and form logic**
   - Match urgency.
   - Keep steps clear.
   - Avoid unnecessary form friction for high-anxiety prospects.

7. **Stress-test the page**
   - Does it sound credible?
   - Does it fit the firm's actual voice?
   - Does it respect the emotional weight of the matter?
   - Does it make a next step easy?

## Required output format

### 1. Conversion Strategy Summary
State the service, target state, and key conversion principle.

### 2. Audience State and Objections
List:
- likely emotional state
- primary fears
- practical objections
- what the page must accomplish psychologically

### 3. Recommended Page Structure
Provide the section-by-section layout with purpose for each section.

### 4. Messaging Guidance
For each major section include:
- what the copy should do
- what tone it should take
- what proof should appear
- what to avoid

### 5. CTA and Form Strategy
Recommend:
- primary CTA
- secondary CTA if needed
- form fields or friction guidance
- call-versus-form considerations

### 6. Trust Assets to Feature
State which existing or missing assets matter most.

### 7. Test Ideas
Recommend 3 to 5 meaningful tests, not cosmetic button-color trivia.

### 8. Risks and Unknowns
Note what could weaken conversion or create compliance risk.

## Quality standards

Strong output should:
- feel grounded and serious
- reduce fear without becoming soft or vague
- distinguish service-specific psychology
- show a believable path from click to consultation
- avoid manipulative fearmongering and inflated claims

## Failure modes to avoid

- writing like a SaaS page or generic personal injury page
- over-indexing on awards and badges while failing to reassure
- using vague empathy with no operational clarity
- making all family law matters sound the same
- recommending generic CRO tricks disconnected from prospect psychology
- burying the CTA or making the next step feel risky

## Example prompts

- Build a paid landing page strategy for a child custody attorney page targeting urgent consultation intent.
- Audit this divorce PPC landing page. Tell me what weakens trust, what confuses the user, and how to restructure it.
- Create a conversion-first outline for a family law landing page targeting high-conflict divorce in this county.

## Universal Guardrails

- Do not fabricate facts about the firm, attorneys, case outcomes, jurisdictions, competitors, or local courts.
- Do not imply a legal guarantee, likely outcome, or universal rule unless the source material explicitly supports it.
- Distinguish observed facts from reasonable marketing inference.
- When evidence is thin, say so and show what additional information would change the recommendation.
- Prefer specific language over inflated language.
- Optimize for business impact, not aesthetic cleverness.
