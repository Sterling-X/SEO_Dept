# Verification Protocol — Full Operational Detail

This file carries the complete Step 1 through Step 9 protocol. SKILL.md holds the condensed workflow; this file governs execution detail. Nothing here is optional.

---

## Step 1: Establish the Governing Framework

Before reviewing individual sentences, identify:

- The controlling jurisdiction
- Whether state, federal, local, tribal, or administrative law applies
- The relevant procedural posture:
  - Initial filing
  - Temporary relief
  - Final adjudication
  - Modification
  - Enforcement
  - Appeal
  - Post-judgment relief
- Whether the rule differs by:
  - Case type
  - Party status
  - Child or adult status
  - Asset type
  - Agreement date
  - Filing date
  - County or judicial circuit
- The current effective date of the governing authority

Do not apply a rule from one procedural stage to another without confirming that it governs both.

---

## Step 2: Extract Every Checkable Legal Claim

Review the page line by line and identify every statement that:

- Says what the law requires, permits, prohibits, presumes, or authorizes
- Describes a legal right, duty, deadline, eligibility rule, exception, remedy, or consequence
- Names or paraphrases a statute, rule, regulation, form, case, or legal doctrine
- Uses an absolute such as:
  - Always
  - Never
  - Must
  - Automatically
  - Only
  - Cannot
  - Guaranteed
  - Required
  - No deadline / no limit / no exception
- Describes:
  - A burden of proof
  - A standard of proof
  - A statutory factor
  - A multi-part test
  - A filing or notice requirement
  - A waiting or residency period
  - A numerical threshold
  - A court's discretion
  - A mandatory outcome
- Uses a jurisdiction-specific legal term
- Describes what happens before, during, or after a court proceeding
- Explains what the firm, court, agency, guardian, evaluator, mediator, or other professional may do

Include legal claims appearing in:

- Introductions
- Headings
- FAQs
- Examples
- Tables
- Calls to action
- Sidebars
- Captions
- Related-content modules

Introductions and FAQs often oversimplify rules that the main body explains more carefully.

**Absolute-language claims get flagged for expanded verification.** Any claim using an absolute term (see list above) is not fully checked once its own citation is confirmed. It also carries into Step 3's absolute-language check, because an absolute statement can be accurate as to its cited provision while still being wrong about the rule as a whole.

---

## Step 3: Verify Each Claim Against Primary Authority

For each material claim:

### Confirm the governing authority

- Read the current text of the statute, rule, regulation, form, or controlling opinion, fetched live during this review session.
- Confirm the section and subsection.
- Confirm the official section title.
- Confirm the effective date.
- Check recent amendments, renumbering, repeals, substitutions, or reorganizations. Official legislature sites usually carry "current through" or recent-acts markers; read them.
- If controlling authorities conflict, the law is unsettled, or courts apply materially different interpretations, do not present one interpretation as universally settled. Explain the conflict, identify the controlling or most authoritative sources, and classify the issue as requiring attorney verification.

### Run a per-claim support test

Ask:

- Does the cited provision govern this exact proposition?
- Does the relevant subsection contain the claimed rule?
- Is the page relying on an adjacent subsection that governs a different issue?
- Is the authority being used outside its intended procedural posture?
- Is the page combining language from multiple provisions into a hybrid standard?
- Does the claim omit an exception or prerequisite that materially changes the rule?

Do not approve a statement merely because the citation points to the correct general statute.

### Absolute-language check (run separately from the per-claim support test)

For every claim using absolute language (always, never, must, automatically, only, cannot, guaranteed, required, no deadline, no exception, and similar):

- Search the **full text** of the cited statute, not just the cited subsection, for a carve-out, exception, or contrary provision.
- Search any other statute governing the same procedural topic (e.g., a companion enforcement or limitations statute) for a provision that qualifies, narrows, or contradicts the absolute claim.
- Do not clear an absolute claim solely because the cited subsection supports it. A citation can be accurate for what it cites and still leave an uncited exception standing elsewhere in the code.
- Affirmatively record that this search was performed and found nothing, or record what it found, in the Verification Log Notes column. Do not silently skip this step because the cited subsection "checked out."

### Test multi-part standards

Confirm:

- Whether elements are connected by "and" or "or"
- Whether every element must be established
- Whether satisfying one element defeats or completes the test
- Whether an exception applies
- Who bears the burden
- What standard of proof applies
- Whether the issue is decided by a judge, jury, agency, or other decision-maker

Do not flatten a multi-part standard into an absolute statement.

### Distinguish legal rules from practice

Classify each relevant statement as one of the following:

- Legal requirement
- Court discretion
- Common practice
- Local procedure
- Strategic recommendation
- Firm process
- Illustrative example

A common practice or litigation strategy must not be presented as a statewide legal requirement.

## Step 4: Completeness and List Testing

When the page summarizes a statutory list, factor test, eligibility test, or set of required contents:

- Read the entire official list.
- Confirm whether the page presents its summary as complete or selective.
- Identify any omitted item that could materially affect a reader.
- Check whether the items are presented in a way that changes their meaning.
- Confirm whether neutral factors are incorrectly described as favoring only one party.
- Check whether exceptions, alternatives, or separate categories have been silently dropped.

If the page uses a selective list, recommend labels such as:

- "Factors most relevant to…"
- "Common considerations include…"
- "Among the issues the court considers…"

---

## Step 5: Procedure, Deadlines, and Local Rules

Verify all claims concerning:

- Venue
- Standing
- Jurisdiction
- Filing requirements
- Required forms
- Service and notice
- Hearings
- Response deadlines
- Waiting periods
- Residency periods
- Evaluation or report deadlines
- Temporary or emergency relief
- Bonds, oaths, letters, certifications, training, reporting, or accountings
- Modification and enforcement procedures
- Appeal or review rights

Determine whether the rule comes from:

- State statute
- Court rule
- Official statewide form
- Administrative regulation
- Local court rule
- Court order
- Common practice

Do not attribute a local or court-ordered requirement to statewide law.

---

## Step 6: Pending Laws and Time-Sensitive Information

For pending bills, recently enacted laws, annual schedules, agency tables, forms, fees, and numerical thresholds:

- Confirm the status as of the review date via the legislature's bill tracker or the agency's official page.
- Use an exact date.
- Distinguish:
  - Introduced
  - Passed one chamber
  - Passed both chambers
  - Sent to the governor
  - Signed
  - Vetoed
  - Effective
- Do not describe a proposed effective date as current law.
- Confirm whether implementation guidance or updated forms have been issued.
- Label pending changes clearly as proposed or not yet effective.

---

## Step 7: Verify Every Citation

For each citation:

- Confirm the authority exists.
- Confirm the citation number and subsection.
- Confirm the official title.
- Confirm the URL resolves to the intended official source (fetch it).
- Confirm the authority directly supports the surrounding sentence.
- Confirm the citation is not being stretched to support unrelated propositions.
- Check whether a more specific provision is required.
- Confirm citation formatting and URL patterns are internally consistent across the page's full source list.
- Flag uncited material legal claims as "Citation or verification needed."
- Do not assume an uncited claim is false merely because it lacks a citation.

Where case law is used:

- Confirm the court and precedential status.
- Confirm the case has not been reversed, vacated, superseded, or limited.
- Distinguish binding authority from persuasive authority.
- Confirm the quoted or paraphrased holding matches the actual decision.

---

## Step 8: Terminology Currency Check

Confirm the page uses the correct jurisdiction-specific terminology. Examples include:

- Custody vs. allocation of parental responsibilities
- Visitation vs. parenting time
- Alimony vs. maintenance
- Placement vs. physical custody
- Conservatorship vs. adult guardianship
- Executor vs. personal representative
- Community property vs. equitable distribution
- Dissolution vs. divorce
- Guardian ad litem vs. child representative vs. attorney for the child

Consumer-recognition or SEO terminology may remain when appropriate, but the statutory term should be introduced where necessary. Flag terminology decisions rather than silently replacing useful consumer language.

Also test technical distinctions such as:

- Waived vs. forfeited
- Eligibility vs. calculation
- Gross income vs. statutory income
- Temporary vs. final order
- Order of appointment vs. letters proving authority
- Decision-making authority vs. records access
- Permitted trust vs. benefit-preserving trust
- Disability as a condition vs. legally relevant conduct
- General rule vs. implementing procedure

---

## Step 9: Ethical and Scope-of-Service Check

Flag language that may misleadingly imply:

- Representation of both sides in an adversarial matter
- Guaranteed results
- Certain outcomes
- Automatic eligibility
- Specialist or certified-expert status without support
- A lawyer-client relationship created by reading the page
- Legal advice tailored to every reader
- Authority to perform services the firm does not offer
- Coverage in a jurisdiction where the firm is not licensed or operating

For mediation, collaborative matters, agreements, or joint processes, confirm that the page accurately distinguishes neutral assistance from representing one party.
