# Service Credit Union Voice -- Canonical Brand Blueprint

**Source:** Service Credit Union Agent Business, Brand, Voice, and Growth Guide (2026.07.19), built from public sources including the official Identity Guidelines v.8 (1.26), press kit, membership page, and regulatory documentation. This skill operationalizes that guide for writers, strategists, and marketing contributors.

**Vertical note:** Service Credit Union is a regulated financial institution, NOT a family law client. The standing family-law production rules do not govern here. What replaces them is heavier: financial advertising is regulated (UDAAP, Truth in Savings, Regulation Z, NCUA, Equal Housing Lender), so **compliance discipline is part of the voice**. A warm sentence with an unverified rate in it is off-brand. Treat every rate, fee, bonus, limit, eligibility rule, and availability claim as a live-verify item, never as something to write from memory or from this skill.

**Internal naming note:** Rocket Clicks refers to this client as "SCU" internally. That is fine for task names, briefs, and internal docs. It is **prohibited in member-facing copy** -- see Section 7.

1. Read this file in full before writing any Service Credit Union content. Do not skim.

## 2. Business Overview

Service Credit Union is a financial cooperative, not a bank. Members are **owners**, not customers -- the $5 Primary Savings deposit is an ownership share, never a fee. The organization began in 1957 serving the Pease Air Force Base community, and that origin gives the military commitment historical credibility: it is a founding identity, not a recent audience-acquisition theme. From there it grew into a large New England credit union with a genuinely distinctive overseas footprint (branches on military bases in Germany, European payment capability) alongside mainstream New Hampshire and Massachusetts retail banking, Grand Forks, first-responder programs, campus and community partnerships (including a 2026 Hockey East partnership), commercial banking, and digital channels.

**The strategic center** is not one rate. It is the combination of: member ownership, military heritage with real overseas infrastructure, New England scale with local community proof, first-responder support, multi-channel access (branch, shared branch, contact center, digital), a broad product ecosystem, international payment capability, and a service model that follows members through changing life stages.

**The central strategic tension:** military heritage is core, but the brand must not feel closed to everyone else. The solution is never to dilute the military story -- it is to organize messaging by need and audience, with a clear eligibility bridge. Military creative should not make nonmilitary eligible readers assume they don't belong.

**Growth posture (RECOMMENDED_STRATEGY, not confirmed internal plan):** deepen New Hampshire primary relationships; grow Massachusetts/New England through credible affinity (first responders, Hockey East, workplace partners); own the military/DoD financial lifecycle (PCS, overseas, return, transition); build density where operating proof exists (Germany, Grand Forks); pursue national digital acquisition selectively, not indiscriminately.

## 3. Fact-Confidence Labels (Non-Negotiable Discipline)

This client's source guide separates fact classes, and this skill preserves that system in place of the usual Observed Fact / Strong Inference / Unknown tiers. Every material claim in a deliverable belongs to one label:

- **`VERIFIED_EVERGREEN`** -- stable, sourced facts (cooperative structure, 1957 Pease origin, voice pillars, member-owner language). Safe to write; recheck for legal docs and major campaigns.
- **`VERIFIED_SNAPSHOT`** -- true as of a date (asset size, member count, branch count, leadership names, community-giving totals, partnerships). Carry an "as of" date in public copy; verify before publication.
- **`DYNAMIC_VERIFY_LIVE`** -- must be checked live before drafting, approval, and publication: rates, APYs, APRs, fees, bonuses, limits, minimums, eligibility details, state availability, terms, branch hours, phone numbers, application links, partner offers, disclosures. **Never write these from memory or from this skill.** Use bracketed placeholders plus a dated-source table.
- **`LEGACY_HYPOTHESIS`** -- 2025 ideas not confirmed as current strategy. The two named traps: **Boston College as a singular priority** (current reality is a broader Hockey East partnership; treat BC as one institution within that ecosystem) and **Delaware first responders** (current first-responder activity is New England; do not put Delaware in strategy language without confirmation).
- **`UNKNOWN_REQUIRE_INTERNAL_CONFIRMATION`** -- budgets, targets, LTV, conversion rates, sponsorship usage rights, product roadmap, approved claims library. State the gap, use a placeholder, name the internal owner needed.

**Never-infer rules (the ones writers actually hit):** never infer an APY from a dividend rate, a fee waiver from "free," universal eligibility from broad digital availability, state availability from a national concept, approval from prequalification, a branch count by adding website lists, a sponsorship right from the existence of a partnership, or a testimonial release from a public social post. Never infer a member's military status, disability, income, or financial condition.

## 4. The Four Voice Pillars

The official voice is **resourceful, invested, open-hearted, bold and confident**. The first three are the character; the fourth is the posture. The voice is not "friendly" -- a merely friendly financial brand is vague and interchangeable. Service Credit Union sounds warm **and** useful, optimistic **and** precise, confident **and** truthful.

**The voice equation:** Human understanding + practical solution + specific proof + clear next step = Service Credit Union voice. Missing understanding reads as product inventory; missing solution reads as sentiment; missing proof reads as promotion; missing next step strands the member.

### Resourceful (makes progress feel possible)
Answer the main question early. Break tasks into steps. Say what the member needs before they're surprised by it. Define terms. Offer an alternative when the primary path doesn't fit. Sentence moves: "Here's what to prepare." / "Start by..." / "The difference is..." / "Before you apply, check..."
**Failure modes:** feature lists with no decision guidance; "contact us" as a substitute for basic explanation; acronyms before definitions; hiding required documents or eligibility.

### Invested (present for more than one transaction)
Connect today's action to the member's longer path. Recognize milestones. Make the member's goal the center. Sentence moves: "As your plans change..." / "Your goal is... Our role is..." / "When it's time to..."
**Failure modes:** declaring loyalty without demonstrating it; treating account opening as the finish line; lifecycle language used to force cross-sell; overpromising relationship outcomes.

### Open-hearted (a person, not a file)
Use "you" and "we." Use contractions. Recognize emotion without dramatizing it. Preserve dignity in hardship, complaints, errors, and credit-building content. Sentence moves: "You don't have to sort it out alone." / "Starting small still counts." / "Something didn't go through. Here's what to check."
**Failure modes:** exclamation-point pileups; fake familiarity; forced humor; youth slang; financial shame; "we know exactly how you feel"; inspirational clichés about service, disability, or hardship; warmth substituting for disclosure.

### Bold and confident (gives direction without becoming absolute)
Active voice. Clear main point. A recommended next step. Real differentiation, acknowledged limits. Sentence moves: "Get clear on the full cost before you refinance." / "Your move is changing. Your access doesn't have to." / "This offer has conditions. Review them before you apply."
**Failure modes:** "best," "lowest," "guaranteed" without substantiation; false certainty; urgency that exploits fear; promises that depend on underwriting; hype words ("revolutionary," "unbeatable," "game-changing").

### Balance by context
The voice stays recognizable; the mix shifts with stakes. Product pages run high Resourceful + high Bold. Membership pages run high across Resourceful, Invested, Open-hearted. Military relocation runs very high Resourceful. First-responder community stories run very high Open-hearted, medium Bold. Fraud alerts run very high Resourceful + very high Bold, low Invested. Application errors run very high Resourceful, high Open-hearted, zero marketing. Paid search runs very high Bold, low Invested/Open-hearted.

## 5. Core Copy Frameworks

### Default content sequence (page logic)
1. **Need** -- name the moment, problem, or goal
2. **Outcome** -- show what progress can look like
3. **Solution** -- the relevant product, service, or relationship
4. **Proof** -- specific, verifiable reasons to trust Service Credit Union
5. **Clarity** -- eligibility, important terms, what the member needs
6. **Next step** -- one primary action, a helpful secondary path
7. **Support** -- how to get human or secure help
8. **Disclosure** -- complete qualifications placed close to the claim

### Need-Bridge-Proof-Next Step (section/module logic)
> Need: "You're moving overseas and still need your U.S. accounts to work around your life."
> Bridge: "Military and international services can help you manage payments, access support, and prepare for the move."
> Proof: "Service Credit Union serves members through Germany locations, international support, and current European payment options."
> Next step: "Review the Germany arrival checklist and confirm which services apply to your assignment."

### Benefit before feature
Never lead with the feature name. "Mobile check deposit" becomes "Deposit an eligible check from your phone, then review current limits, holds, and availability before relying on the funds." Note the pattern: the benefit sentence carries its own condition.

### Value-proposition laddering
Connect at least two levels; never jump straight to emotion without functional proof. Level 1 functional (save, borrow, pay, move money) -> Level 2 experiential (clarity, fewer steps, human help, continuity across moves) -> Level 3 emotional (progress, stability, belonging) -> Level 4 mission (member ownership, military and first-responder support, community action).

### Call-and-response headlines (use sparingly)
Formula: "You [real action or moment]. We [specific support]." Examples: "You plan the move. We help keep your money moving." / "You found the home. We help you understand the financing." Reserve for campaign platforms, major lifecycle pages, and high-value transitions -- it loses force when every headline uses it.

### Headline families
Outcome ("Take control of your next financial move"), Need ("Banking built for a life that moves"), Decision ("Choose the checking account that fits how you bank"), Education ("Understand the full cost before you refinance"), Community ("Local action. Lasting impact."), Membership ("Join a credit union built around its members"), Military ("Keep your financial life connected through every move"), Security ("Stop. Verify. Protect your account."). Every headline must be matched by true body copy and approved claims.

### CTA system
Primary: Become a Member, Open an Account, Apply Now, Check Current Rates, Compare Accounts, Find a Location, Talk With a Specialist. Education: Learn How It Works, See What You'll Need, Understand the Costs, Compare the Options. Support: Get Secure Help, Chat With Us, Call Member Service.
Rules: one dominant CTA per page state; button text names the action; never "Click Here"; distinguish "Check Rates," "Get Prequalified," "Apply," and "Open" (they are different commitments); never call a form "Get Started" when it creates a hard credit inquiry; tell users when a link leaves the site.

## 6. Tone Spectrum by Situation

- **Everyday progress:** optimistic, practical, clear. "Build a stronger savings habit with an account that matches how often you need access."
- **Major milestone (home, transition):** encouraging, steady, informed. Never breathless.
- **Financial stress:** calm, dignified, nonjudgmental. "Review the available support early, before the problem grows." Never imply a specific assistance outcome.
- **Security or fraud:** urgent, factual, controlled. "Do not reply or share account information. Contact Service Credit Union through a verified secure channel." Never include a login link in a message warning against links.
- **Service outage:** transparent, accountable, action oriented -- and only when operationally true.
- **Complaint / service recovery:** listening, accountable, specific. Sequence: recognize -> reflect -> state what's known -> act -> verified next step. Never admit legal liability, blame the member, promise reimbursement, or use a cheerful promotional tone.
- **Decline or ineligibility:** respectful, clear, legally reviewed. No shame, no argument, no circumvention tips.
- **Community tragedy:** human, restrained, service centered. No product marketing attached. Ever.

## 7. Nomenclature and Terminology

### Required
- **Service Credit Union** (preferred full name); **Service CU** (after first reference or where space is tight)
- **Service Federal Credit Union** -- contracts and legally binding documents only
- **member / member-owner** -- never "customer" (exception: describing another institution's model, quoting, or third-party legal context)
- Primary Savings; share certificate; **dividends** (deposit products at credit unions pay dividends -- "interest" is for loans and some educational comparisons)
- Define on first use: APY, APR, HELOC, PCS (permanent change of station), DoD, NCUA, SEPA, IBAN, escrow, rate lock, variable rate, draw period, repayment period

### Prohibited in member-facing copy
- **SCU** and **SFCU** (internal shorthand only)
- "the credit union's customers"
- "guaranteed savings"; "instant approval" (unless literally, legally true); "risk-free"; "safe" as an absolute
- "free" without validating all conditions under Truth in Savings
- "no hidden fees" as a substitute for listing fees
- "preapproved" when the process is prequalification
- "nationwide eligibility" without current approval; "local" where there is no local presence or proof

### Style mechanics
- Sentences average 12-22 words for web; paragraphs one idea, two to four sentences; front-load meaning
- Active voice; natural contractions; Oxford comma; sentence case for headings and buttons unless the design system says otherwise
- Em dashes sparingly; no semicolon-heavy web copy; no all-caps emphasis; no stacked exclamation points
- Numerals for amounts, dates, rates, terms; "$5", "$10,000"; unambiguous dates ("July 19, 2026"); no "today"/"soon" in evergreen copy
- Rates: distinguish rate, dividend rate, APY, APR; pair "as low as" with qualification and disclosure; identify fixed vs. variable; include "as of" dates; never calculate from memory; never copy a rate from a cached page
- Reading level: roughly grades 6-9 for everyday/membership pages; technical products may use technical terms with immediate plain-language definitions

### Inclusive language
Do not assume marriage, children, gender, homeownership, citizenship, rank, disability, or income. Military: distinguish service member, veteran, DoD employee, and family; never assume combat; never use rank without relevance and permission. First responders: use the profession accurately; include dispatchers and EMS where the program does; never use one profession's imagery for another. Disability: no "inspirational" framing based on disability alone.

## 8. Compliance Guardrails (Load-Bearing, Not Fine Print)

This is where this client differs most from the rest of the book. Compliance failures are voice failures.

- **UDAAP overall impression:** a claim can be literally true and still deceptive if material conditions are hidden, delayed, or visually minimized. Review headline + image + offer + conditions + CTA + landing page as one impression. A footnote cannot rescue a misleading headline.
- **Conditions live next to claims.** Never separate a key limitation from the benefit it qualifies. Never make a conditional result sound certain. Never present a best case as typical.
- **Deposit claims (Truth in Savings):** "free," "no fee," "bonus," "up to," "APY," "no minimum" are regulated words. Each needs current product verification and complete conditions.
- **Credit claims (Regulation Z):** stating an APR, payment, term, or "as low as" can trigger required disclosures. Payment examples need complete assumptions. Never promise approval; never guarantee savings; never imply no cost when cost is deferred or financed.
- **Membership vs. credit approval are separate.** Joining never guarantees a loan, a rate, a limit, or underwriting preference. Prequalification is not approval.
- **Eligibility is real.** Never summarize membership as "everyone can join." Current public paths: military/veteran + family, DoD employment + family, workplace partners, and association pathways (verify live). The $5 deposit is an ownership share, framed as ownership, never as a fee.
- **Testimonials:** real person, written permission, accurate experience, current product. Never invent a member quote or composite story. Placeholder testimonials must be unmistakably labeled nonpublishable.
- **Community claims:** verify amount, period, recipient, geography, and whether figures are cumulative before publishing. Use the community-proof formula: Need -> Action -> Partner -> Place -> Scale -> Outcome -> Continuity. "We care deeply about every community" without evidence is off-brand.
- **Fair lending:** flag any housing, mortgage, home-equity, auto, card, or personal-loan targeting/geography strategy for specialized review. No occupation-based messaging that implies different credit standards.
- **Investment/insurance separation:** never place NCUA insurance language where it could imply investment or insurance protection.
- **Accessibility:** the public target is WCAG 2.2 Level AA. One H1, logical heading order, descriptive links, defined acronyms, meaningful alt text, readable disclosures, instructions not dependent on color alone.
- **Privacy/security:** never request credentials, one-time codes, or full SSN/card numbers through content; route account-specific questions to secure channels; never reveal internal fraud controls.

**Fatal flaws (deliverable fails immediately):** invented financial term or rate; expired offer; promised approval; omitted material condition; fabricated testimonial; misused NCUA insurance language; misleading overall impression.

## 9. Audience Quick Reference

- **Active-duty service members:** center continuity through PCS, deployment, and overseas assignment. Very high Resourceful. Conditions (active-duty status, direct deposit, program dates) live next to any benefit claim.
- **Military families:** the household manages the move; write to the family's logistics, not just the member's service.
- **Veterans and transitioning members:** transition is a financial lifecycle stage (benefits, employment change, relocation home). No assumptions of hardship or of stability.
- **DoD employees and families:** a distinct eligibility path -- name it; do not fold them into "military" generically.
- **Overseas / Germany members:** the most operationally distinctive audience. Real proof: base branches, SEPA, international bill pay, currency services. Germany content needs localization review (currency notation, dialing, dates, holidays); machine translation is never final copy.
- **New Hampshire households:** the home market. Local proof is abundant and should be specific -- HQ, branches, nonprofits, workplace partners. This is where "local" is actually true.
- **Massachusetts / New England households:** growth market via credible affinity (Hockey East, first responders, workplace partners), not generic regional ads.
- **First responders:** center the person and community, never the brand. Include police, fire, paramedic, EMT, and dispatch where programs do. Never place a hard-sell CTA next to a tragedy or health-risk story; never turn occupational risk into sales urgency. Current geography: New England.
- **Students / young adults / campus (Hockey East):** community-first, useful education, no youth slang, no "adulting." Boston College is one institution inside the Hockey East ecosystem, not a confirmed standalone strategy.
- **Homebuyers / homeowners:** education-heavy, steady tone; full-cost honesty ("Understand the full cost before you refinance") is on-brand differentiation.
- **Savers / credit builders:** dignity is the rule. "Building credit takes time. A product designed for an earlier stage can help you begin, but costs, limits, and responsible repayment still matter."
- **Small businesses / commercial:** relationship framing -- "commercial tools and a relationship built around the way your business operates." High Resourceful + Bold.

## 11. What to Avoid (Anti-Patterns)

- **Generic bank-speak:** "innovative solutions," "one-stop shop," "world-class service," "seamless" without evidence, "unlock your potential," "empower" in every paragraph, "financial journey" repeated without specifics.
- **Hype:** unbeatable, revolutionary, guaranteed, instant, effortless, risk-free, perfect, best, lowest, "everyone qualifies."
- **Empty community language:** "we're proud to give back" without action; "community is everything" without place or outcome; "local people serving local people" on a page with no local fact.
- **Institutional distance:** "the applicant shall," "customers must," "users are required to" where "you'll need" is clearer; passive voice that hides responsibility.
- **False empathy:** "we know exactly how you feel"; "don't worry"; "it's no big deal."
- **Cutesy money language:** "adulting," "treat yo' self," "money moves" as slang, piggy-bank clichés, jokes about debt or credit scores.
- **Militarized marketing:** "warrior" as a label; "battle high fees," "arsenal," "mission accomplished"; camouflage as a substitute for relevance; implying combat experience.
- **The calibration pair:** weak military copy sounds like "Battle high fees with our military banking arsenal." Aligned: "Your assignment may change. Your financial access shouldn't. Review current military, relocation, and international services built to support life across locations."
- **Hero-washing first responders:** "We proudly salute our hometown heroes with unbeatable deals." Aligned: specific programs, practical resources, eligibility -- respect through usefulness.

## 13. Brand Operating Rules

### 10 Rules for Writing in This Brand

1. **Open on the member's moment, not the institution.** Name the need, the move, the milestone, or the problem first. "At Service Credit Union, we..." is not an opener.
2. **Run the voice equation in every section.** Human understanding + practical solution + specific proof + clear next step. If one is missing, the section isn't done.
3. **Members are owners.** "Member" or "member-owner," never "customer." The $5 deposit is an ownership share. The cooperative difference is identity, not trivia -- but never flatten it to "basically a bank with better rates."
4. **Never invent a number.** No rate, fee, APY, APR, bonus, limit, minimum, term, count, or eligibility rule from memory. Placeholder + dated live source, always. This is the single most important rule in the skill.
5. **Keep conditions welded to claims.** The qualifier travels with the benefit in the same sentence or visual unit. Footnotes don't rescue headlines.
6. **Warm and precise at the same time.** Contractions, "you" and "we," dignity in hardship -- and defined terms, disclosed conditions, honest limits. Warmth is never a substitute for disclosure.
7. **One dominant CTA, honestly labeled.** Distinguish Check Rates / Get Prequalified / Apply / Open. Never disguise a hard credit inquiry as "Get Started."
8. **Define everything on first use.** APY, APR, HELOC, PCS, SEPA, share certificate, dividend, escrow, draw period. Grades 6-9 for everyday pages; plain language is not oversimplification.
9. **Benefit before feature, proof before emotion.** Ladder from functional value to emotional value; never jump to emotion without functional proof underneath it.
10. **Respect identity; never costume it.** Military and first-responder content earns trust through operational usefulness (PCS logistics, Germany infrastructure, shift-worker realities), never through camouflage theming, "warrior" labels, or hero-discount framing.

### 10 Rules for Staying Strategically Aligned

1. **The brand is the capable guide with a human pulse.** Every piece should make progress feel possible and the next step clear -- solving, not posturing.
2. **The differentiation is the combination.** Ownership + military heritage with real overseas infrastructure + New England community proof + product breadth + multi-channel access. Never hang the brand on one rate.
3. **Military heritage is a founding identity, not a theme.** 1957, Pease Air Force Base, Germany bases. Use the origin for continuity ("service shaped the operating model"), never as nostalgia disconnected from present value.
4. **Keep the eligibility bridge visible.** Military distinctiveness must not read as a closed door. Organize by need and audience; make the pathways (military/veteran, DoD, workplace, association) clear without claiming "everyone can join."
5. **New Hampshire is depth; New England is affinity; Germany and Grand Forks are proof; national digital is selective.** Match the content investment to the tier. Generic regional advertising is not the Massachusetts strategy -- credible communities are.
6. **Hockey East is the frame; Boston College is a legacy hypothesis.** Do not build BC-specific campaigns or claims without confirmed rights and internal priority. Same for Delaware first responders -- current programs are New England.
7. **Community claims follow the proof formula.** Need, action, partner, place, verified scale, outcome, continuity. Lead with action; the brand statement is the weakest form of evidence.
8. **Measure relationships, not form fills.** The strategic language is funded, activated, retained relationships -- direct deposit, digital activation, retention through relocation -- not application volume.
9. **Compliance is product quality.** UDAAP, Truth in Savings, Reg Z, fair lending, NCUA language, accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA) are part of the deliverable, not a downstream department. Flag targeting and geography strategies for lending products.
10. **Expose uncertainty instead of polishing over it.** State gaps, label facts, name the internal owner needed. Durable trust beats short-term click-through -- build content that helps a person decide, not merely content that ranks.

### 10 Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Writing "SCU" in member-facing copy.** It's the internal client name and it's prohibited in the brand guidance. Service Credit Union or Service CU only.
2. **Calling members "customers."** The single fastest way to sound like every bank the brand is positioned against.
3. **Inventing or "remembering" a rate, fee, or bonus.** Includes copying from cached pages or search results. Fatal flaw, not a style issue.
4. **Treating the $5 as a fee.** "Pay $5 to access our products" misrepresents the cooperative relationship. It's the ownership share.
5. **Claiming "everyone can join."** A broad pathway is not the absence of eligibility. This is both a trust and a compliance failure.
6. **Implying membership means approval.** Membership, application, underwriting, and funding are separate stages. Prequalification is not preapproval.
7. **Militarized clichés and hero-discount framing.** "Battle fees," "arsenal," "salute our heroes with unbeatable deals" -- these exploit identity instead of serving it.
8. **Empty community warmth.** Giving-back language with no verified action, place, or outcome attached. Use the proof formula or cut it.
9. **Reviving legacy hypotheses as fact.** Boston College standalone campaigns and Delaware expansion are 2025 ideas, not confirmed strategy.
10. **Separating conditions from claims.** Best-case examples presented as typical, "free" with unstated requirements, urgency that exploits fear, or a headline the footnote contradicts. The overall impression is what's regulated -- and what's on-brand.
