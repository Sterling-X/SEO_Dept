# SEO and AI-Search Principles

- Separate practice-area hubs, procedural pages, situational pages, physical-office location pages, and location-specific practice pages.
- Assess search intent and existing content before recommending a new page.
- Do not assume keyword overlap proves cannibalization. Check intent, ranking behavior, internal targeting, and available performance evidence.
- Distinguish crawlability, indexability, canonicalization, ranking, and conversion. A problem in one layer does not prove a problem in another.
- Distinguish verified physical office locations from service-area coverage.
- Preserve a client's explicitly defined reporting segments, but label custom definitions clearly.
- Keep Google Search Console, GA4, map visibility, call tracking, and CRM measures separate. They describe different parts of the acquisition and intake journey.
- Treat correlations and ranking-system theories as hypotheses unless evidence supports a stronger conclusion.
- Recheck changeable client facts and platform claims before relying on them.
- Keep jurisdiction-specific legal content aligned with verified primary or authoritative sources.
- Prioritize work by expected impact, evidence, effort, dependencies, and time to a measurable signal.
- Treat AI-search recommendations as testable strategies, not guaranteed citation or ranking mechanisms.
