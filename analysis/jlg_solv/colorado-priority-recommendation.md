# JLG Colorado: which location to focus on, and why

Prepared 2026-09-18. Answers: which Colorado location has the best chance of increasing
leads and local dominance fastest, and which comes second.

## Recommendation

1. **Colorado Springs** — first.
2. **Denver metro** — second, as a configuration recovery first and a prominence campaign second.
3. **Not Fort Collins first.** This corrects the earlier brief, which ranked it #1.

## Evidence 1: where the business actually comes from

Leads and funded agreements by phone area code, 2026-03-01 to 2026-09-18
(`all_firms_offline_conversion`, JLG):

| Region | Leads | Consults scheduled | Funded agreements |
|---|---|---|---|
| 303/720 Denver metro | 1,080 | 441 | **559** |
| other / out of state | 788 | 283 | 272 |
| 719 Colorado Springs / south CO | 365 | 145 | **118** |
| 970 Fort Collins / north CO | 331 | 116 | **89** |
| 307 Wyoming | 282 | 112 | 65 |
| Chicago | 33 | 9 | 6 |
| South Florida | 16 | 9 | 7 |

Denver metro is half of all funded agreements. Colorado Springs produces 33% more hires
than Fort Collins on a similar lead count.

## Evidence 2: rank has to clear ~9 before top-three share exists

Empirical curve across all 36 location x term pairs in the account, June onward:

| Average grid rank | Top-three share |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | 100% |
| 2–3 | 80% |
| 3–5 | 43% |
| 7–9 | 14% |
| 9–11 | 3% |
| 11–13 | 0.5% |
| 13–15 | 0.7% |
| 15+ | 0.1% |

The payoff is steeply non-linear and effectively nothing exists above rank 11. Half the
account's tracked term-location pairs (18 of 36) sit above rank 13, where share is ~0.

## Evidence 3: current position of each Colorado location

| Location | Generic terms | City-name terms | Footprint | Distance to payoff zone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commerce City | rank 1.8, 83% share | rank 1.0, 100% | 99% | none, saturated |
| Colorado Springs | **rank 7.8, 18% share** | rank 14.9, 0.3% | 44% | **already inside it** |
| Englewood | rank 13.9, 0% | rank 4.0, 54% | 63% | ~5 positions on generic |
| Fort Collins | rank 14.4, 1% | rank 15.2, 0.4% | 71% | **~6 to enter, ~10 for real share** |
| Denver | rank 15.3, 0% | rank 13.8, 0% | ~7% | ~6 to enter, hardest market |

## Why Colorado Springs is first

1. **It is the only under-performing location already inside the payoff zone.** At rank 7.8
   on "divorce lawyers" it earns 18.2% share. Moving ~3 positions into the 3–5 band
   historically corresponds to ~43% share — roughly a doubling to tripling, not a
   step from zero.
2. **The market converts.** 365 leads and 118 funded agreements in 6.5 months, second only
   to Denver metro, and more hires than Fort Collins.
3. **There is a specific, diagnosable defect rather than a general competitive deficit.**
   It ranks ~8 on generic searches but ~15 on "colorado springs divorce lawyers", earning
   0.3% share on the highest-intent local term in its own market. That inversion is
   abnormal and consistent across 16 scans. A named defect is faster to fix than grinding
   up a competitive ladder. Leading hypothesis: the pack for explicit "colorado springs"
   queries anchors near the city centre while the office sits northwest on Chuckwagon Road.
   Untested; category and city-page relevance are alternative causes.
4. **Competition is materially lighter than Denver metro**, so position gains are cheaper
   per unit of movement.
5. **Headroom exists.** 44% footprint means over half the grid has no presence at all,
   unlike Commerce City at 99%.

## Why Denver metro is second, and framed as recovery

1. **It is worth the most.** 559 of 1,121 funded agreements. A 10% gain here outweighs a
   50% gain in Fort Collins.
2. **The near-term opportunity is recovery, not conquest.** Denver's footprint fell 25% to
   ~7% in mid-July, one scan after the Englewood listing became a Service Area Business.
   Restoring something recently lost is faster and cheaper than winning new ground, and it
   may be a listing-configuration fix.
3. **But recovery alone will not produce leads from the map.** Denver held 25% footprint in
   February with 0% top-three share at the same rank ~15. Footprint recovery restores
   presence, not placement. Pack placement in Denver is a longer prominence campaign, and
   Casey's read that Denver is very competitive is consistent with rank ~15 held flat all
   year despite 224 reviews, the most of any location.
4. **Sequence matters:** fix configuration, re-scan to confirm, then decide on prominence
   investment with evidence rather than before it.

## Why not Fort Collins first (correcting the earlier brief)

The earlier brief ranked Fort Collins #1 and claimed "roughly four positions" of
improvement would start converting. Both were wrong:

- **The distance is ~6 positions to enter the shallow payoff band and ~10 for real share**,
  not 4. At rank 14.4–15.2 it sits in the dead zone.
- **Its 71% footprint is worth little at rank 15.** Footprint without rank does not convert;
  the account's own curve shows 0.1–0.7% share above rank 13.
- **Its GBP has never produced a tracked lead.** Location-tagged call records show
  Denver 885, Commerce City 866, Colorado Springs 598, Englewood 214 GBP-sourced calls in
  2024 and **zero for Fort Collins in any year** — either no tracking number was ever
  assigned or the listing produces no calls. Its 331 leads come from other channels.
- Fort Collins remains the best *story* (largest measured improvement) and is worth
  continuing, but it is not the fastest route to leads.

## Limits on this recommendation

- **Area code is a proxy for location, not a location.** Mobile numbers travel; 788 leads
  (27%) carry out-of-state numbers. Regional splits carry real error.
- **The three funnel stages are event counts, not a strict funnel** — Denver metro shows 559
  funded agreements against 441 consults scheduled, so hires are recorded without a
  matching consult event. Do not compute conversion rates from this table.
- **The Denver metro bucket contains three offices.** 303/720 covers the Denver, Englewood
  and Commerce City listings, so this data cannot attribute metro leads to a single office.
- **Lead counts are all-channel**, not GBP-attributed. Map-share gains are not guaranteed to
  produce proportional lead gains.
- **Per-office GBP call attribution is 10 months stale.** `johnson_law_group.call_data` ends
  2025-12-03, and all GBP calls carry `medium = direct`, so brand-driven and
  discovery-driven calls cannot be separated. Denver's 2024 call lead may partly reflect
  brand searches on the flagship listing rather than map discovery.
- **The rank-to-share curve is built on 36 pairs from one account** and mixes markets of
  different competitive density. Treat band midpoints as indicative, not predictive.

# Action plan, and a correction to my own reasoning

Added 2026-09-18 after two further checks.

## Correction: the Colorado Springs city-name gap is not the quick fix

The recommendation above called the city-name inversion "a named defect, faster to fix than
grinding up a competitive ladder." Two checks weaken that.

**Check 1 — absence, not just low rank.** Colorado Springs on its city-name terms has
footprint **27.6%** ("colorado springs divorce lawyers") and **38.4%** ("colorado springs
family lawyers"), against 51-59% on generic terms. Adding the city name to the query
roughly halves where it appears at all *and* pushes rank from 7.8 to 14.9. Since the grid,
the office and the competitor set are identical across both queries, the only variable is
the query string: explicit-geo queries are resolved city-wide rather than by proximity to
the searcher, so the whole grid competes against the strongest firms in Colorado Springs
instead of just the nearby ones. **That is competitive displacement, not a fixable on-page
defect.**

**Check 2 — the comparison that settles it.** Fort Collins has **84.9-86.5%** footprint on
its own city-name terms, with a similar office-to-centroid offset (office 40.5237/-105.0302
against a city centre roughly 5 miles NW; Colorado Springs office 38.9144/-104.8657 against
a centre roughly 5.6 miles SE). Similar geometry, radically different city-name footprint.
The discriminator is competitive density (Colorado Springs metro is roughly 4x Fort Collins),
not page quality or proximity alone.

So the earlier "pack anchors near the city centre" hypothesis is only part of it, and the
city-name term is the **harder** fight in Colorado Springs, not the easier one.

## Correction: the website is not the lever for this metric

Verified live: all five Colorado location pages exist at `/colorado/locations/{city}/`
(the old flat `/colorado-springs-divorce-lawyer/` now 301-redirects, consistent with the
March-April 2026 cutover). The Colorado Springs page is **not thin**: title "Family Lawyer
in Colorado Springs | Johnson Law Group", H1 "Colorado Springs Family Law Attorneys",
~900-1,000 words, full street address and phone, 15 linked practice areas, four named
testimonials, eight named neighbourhoods (Flying Horse, Northgate, The Broadmoor, Skyway,
Peregrine, Woodmen, Briargate, Cordera), map link.

**SoLV measures the map pack, which is driven by the Google Business Profile — not by the
website page.** Building or expanding city pages will not move this metric. Note also that
the page targets "family lawyer in Colorado Springs" and the listing ranks 15.72 on exactly
that phrase, which is further evidence the page is not the constraint.

One real gap found: **office hours are not stated on the page.** Worth confirming hours are
complete on the GBP itself, which does matter.

## Revised plan

### 1. Colorado Springs — target the generic terms, not the city-name terms

The asset is "divorce lawyers" at rank **7.77** (18.2% share, 59% footprint) and "family
lawyers" at rank **8.9** (8.9% share, 51% footprint). These are the only non-saturated
positions inside the payoff zone anywhere in the account. The account's own curve puts the
3-5 rank band at 43% share, so roughly three positions of movement is a doubling to
tripling — in a market that produced 365 leads and 118 funded agreements in 6.5 months.

Levers, in the order the evidence supports:

1. **GBP configuration audit** (requires dashboard access I do not have): confirm the
   primary category is the highest-intent available, that services, hours and photos are
   complete, and that no duplicate or suspended listing exists. This is the layer the
   metric actually measures.
2. **Footprint expansion.** 41-49% of the grid has no presence even on generic terms. That
   is a prominence problem, addressed by citations, local links and brand authority.
3. **Reviews as support, not lead lever.** 89 reviews at 4.06, against Commerce City's 212
   at 4.09. Worth closing, but explicitly *not* established as causal here: Denver carries
   224 reviews and the worst visibility in the account.
4. **Do not fund website work for this metric.** It will move organic, not the pack.

### 2. Denver metro — recovery, then a decision point

1. Resolve the Service Area Business question on the Englewood listing and restore the
   street address if the conversion was unintentional.
2. Re-scan Denver and Englewood. If Denver's footprint recovers toward 25%, the cause is
   confirmed and cheap.
3. **Then stop and decide.** At rank ~15 with 224 reviews, Denver pack placement is a long
   prominence campaign. Do not commit that budget before the recovery test returns evidence.

### 3. The larger reframe Casey should weigh

Commerce City's dominance (99% footprint, rank 1.8) is best explained by proximity in a
low-competition suburb, not by superior optimisation — nothing in the GBP data distinguishes
its configuration from Denver's, which has comparable reviews and fails. If the map-pack
ceiling in Denver and Colorado Springs is set mainly by density and proximity, then
**map-pack work is the wrong primary channel for those two metros**, and the fastest lead
gains there are more likely in organic and paid. Map-pack effort returns most where
proximity already favours the firm. This is a hypothesis consistent with the five-location
pattern, not a demonstrated finding.

## What still needs checking, and by whom

- GBP dashboard: categories, hours, services, duplicate/suspended listings for Colorado
  Springs and Denver. Not visible in BigQuery.
- Whether the Englewood SAB conversion was intentional.
- Competitor identity and strength across the Colorado Springs and Denver grids. Not in
  this dataset; needs a competitor-level grid tool.
- Current organic performance for these city terms. The 2026 GSC layer was not loaded here.

# Review corrections, 2026-09-18 (round 1 on this deliverable)

Independent `seo-reviewer` pass: 5 blockers, 11 material findings. It agreed the ordering
is right and argued most of my stated reasoning was not. Verified each against BigQuery.

## The finding that reframes the whole recommendation

**Map-pack share does not drive calls in this account.** GBP calls, Jan-Jul 2026, against
map position:

| Location | Top-three share | Footprint | GBP calls |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commerce City | ~90% | 99% | 191 |
| Cheyenne WY | **0%** | 87% | **134** |
| Denver | **0%** | **7%** | **115** |
| Colorado Springs | 6.8% | 44% | 88 |
| Englewood | 21% | 63% | 32 |
| Fort Collins | 0.75% | 71% | 30 |
| Chicago | 2.2% | 20% | 28 |
| Boca Raton | 0% | 0% | 3 |

Denver has the worst map visibility in Colorado and produces **3.6x the calls of
Englewood**, which has the second-best share. Cheyenne produces 134 calls at zero share.
Only Boca Raton, which is absent entirely, is genuinely starved.

Reinforced by the isolable area codes: 719 (6.8% share) 365 leads, 970 (0.75%) 331,
307 (0.0%) 282 — a roughly 9x share spread against a 1.3x lead spread.

Commerce City is the only case where high share coincides with high calls, and it is also
a low-competition suburb where proximity does the work rather than optimisation.

**Conclusion: market size and brand presence drive calls here; map-pack share is a weak
secondary factor.** This bounds the upside of any map-share work and it means the
prioritisation must answer two separate questions, which the original conflated:

- **Fastest lead growth: Denver metro.** 1,108 leads and 561 funded agreements, and the
  listing already produces 115 calls at near-zero map visibility. The constraint there is
  not map share.
- **Fastest map dominance: Colorado Springs.** Still correct, with modest expected lead
  effect.
