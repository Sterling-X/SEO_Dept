# Johnson Law Group — Colorado local map visibility, 2026 year to date

Prepared 18 September 2026. Covers the five Colorado Google Business Profile locations.
Data: Local Brand Manager grid scans (11 Feb – 14 Sep 2026) and Google Business Profile
performance (1 Jan – 14 Sep 2026), via the SterlingX warehouse.

## The short version

One location has genuinely improved this year: **Fort Collins**, which went from appearing
in about a fifth of its local map area to appearing in roughly 71% of it. That is a real
and substantial gain, and it is invisible in the headline visibility score, for reasons
explained below.

**Englewood** is currently at its strongest point of the year, though only narrowly ahead
of where it was in March. **Commerce City** has been dominant and flat all year.
**Colorado Springs** improved in early March and has been unchanged for six months.
**Denver** has gone backwards and now barely appears on the map at all.

Across all five locations, the firm is being seen more on Google and contacted less.

## How to read the numbers

The tool drops a 13x13 grid of points across each office's market and checks, from each
point, whether the firm appears in Google's top three map results. Three measures come
out of it:

- **Top-three share** — the percentage of grid points where the firm makes the top three.
  This is the headline "share of local voice" figure.
- **Map footprint** — the percentage of grid points where the firm appears *at all*.
- **Average position** — where it ranks at the points where it does appear.

**The top-three share has a hard cut-off, and that matters.** A location can grow its
footprint enormously and improve its average position while its top-three share stays
near zero, because third place is a cliff edge. Fort Collins is exactly that case. Judging
these locations on top-three share alone would report its best result of the year as no
change at all.

One precision note: the source data records top-three share in whole percentage points.
Any location sitting below about 1% is at the limit of what the tool can measure, so
Fort Collins' and Denver's top-three figures should be read as "effectively zero" with no
readable trend, not as small real movements.

## Location by location

| Location | Top-three share | Map footprint | Average position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commerce City | 92 Apr → **92** Sep (flat, 89–94 all year) | 99% all year | 1.8 |
| Englewood | 22 Feb → **27** Sep (best month, just above Mar 26) | ~63% flat | 9.4 → 8.7 |
| Colorado Springs | 3 Feb → **8** Sep (flat since March) | 45% → 47% | 13.2 → 12.1 |
| Fort Collins | effectively zero all year | **21.5% → 71.2%** | 17.5 → 14.9 |
| Denver | zero all year | **25% → under 10%** | ~15 (unchanged) |

**Fort Collins — the one substantial improvement.** Its footprint climbed steadily from
21.5% in February to 71.2% in September, measured across roughly fifteen separate scans,
and its average position improved from 17.5 to 14.9. This is a location that went from
largely invisible to broadly present. The honest caveat: position ~15 is still far outside
the top three, so almost none of that presence converts into top-three visibility yet. All
six of its tracked searches sit between 0% and 2.5% top-three share. It is real progress
that is still one significant step away from paying off.

**Englewood — strongest month, but not a breakthrough.** Its September average of 27% is
its best of 2026, though March reached 26% and peaked at 28% on a single scan, so the
current level is a return to and slight improvement on where it was six months ago rather
than new ground. Average position improved from 9.4 to 8.7.

**Commerce City — at the ceiling, holding.** It appears somewhere in 99% of its grid and
averages position 1.8. It has run between 89% and 94% top-three share all year with no
trend in either direction. September (92%) is inside April's range (90–94%). There is no
meaningful headroom here. This is a result to protect, not improve.

**Colorado Springs — improved once, in early March.** Top-three share rose from 3% to
roughly 8% and average position from 13.2 to 12.1, but essentially all of that landed by
2 March and it has been flat for six months. September's 8% sits mid-range against
March's 8–11%. Its footprint has not grown this year.

**Denver — the problem.** Denver has never registered top-three share in 2026, and its
footprint has fallen from 25% of the grid in February to under 10% now. It ranks around
position 15 where it appears, and it now appears almost nowhere. This is the largest of
the five markets and the weakest result.

## The most useful finding: these offices only win when searchers name their city

Splitting each location's tracked searches into those that include the city name
("englewood divorce lawyers") and those that do not ("divorce lawyers") changes the
picture substantially. All five Colorado locations track the same structure — two
city-name searches and the same two generic searches — so this comparison is sound.

Top-three share, September:

| Location | City-name searches | Generic searches |
|---|---|---|
| Commerce City | 100% | 83% |
| Englewood | 54% | **0%** |
| Colorado Springs | **0.3%** | 15% |
| Fort Collins | 0.5% | 1% |
| Denver | 0% | 0% |

Three things follow:

**Englewood's success is entirely city-name driven.** It takes 54% of the top-three slots
when someone types "englewood family lawyers" (where it appears at every single grid point
at position 3.6), and 0% when they type "divorce lawyers" — all year, without exception.
Its September gain came entirely from city-name searches rising from 38% to 54%. That is
an improvement in a category it already won, not a breakthrough into the larger generic
market.

**Colorado Springs is backwards, and this is worth investigating.** It takes 15% of
top-three slots for generic "divorce lawyers" and "family lawyers" searches, but
essentially **zero** for "colorado springs divorce lawyers" and "colorado springs family
lawyers." It ranks around position 8 on generic searches and position 15 on its own city
name. That is the opposite of the normal pattern and it is consistent across sixteen
scans, so it is not noise. The likely explanation is that explicit "colorado springs"
searches return a map pack anchored near the city centre, while the office sits in the
northwest of the city at 2139 Chuckwagon Road, so nearby competitors win those searches.
This is the single most specific and testable problem in the Colorado data.

**Only Commerce City competes on generic searches**, and it is the smallest of these
markets. In the metros that generate the most enquiries, the firm holds no top-three map
presence for unqualified "divorce lawyers" searches at all.

## Why Denver declined, and the decision it forces

Denver's footprint halved in a single week: 15.1% on 13 July, 7.1% on 20 July, and it has
stayed near 7% every week since.

On that same 13 July scan, the Englewood listing changed from a street address to a
**"Service Area Business"** — a listing type with no public street address that covers a
broad radius instead. The two offices are about 11 miles apart and their grids overlap
heavily. Google normally shows only one listing per business for a given search, so the
leading explanation is that the broadened Englewood listing is now winning that filter
across much of Denver and suppressing the Denver office listing.

**This is a hypothesis, not a proven cause.** For it: the change and the drop are one week
apart and the drop has held for nine weeks. Against it: Englewood's own footprint did not
grow when Denver's fell, which a simple trade-off would predict. A coincident Google local
update would fit the evidence about as well.

Either way, one question needs answering first: **was the Englewood listing deliberately
converted to a Service Area Business?** Englewood is a real office at 385 Inverness
Parkway. Converting a genuine office to an address-less service-area listing is usually a
mistake, and it can also happen through a failed address verification. If it was not
intentional, restoring the street address is the highest-priority fix in Colorado.

## Why calls are down even though visibility is up

Comparing two equal three-month windows — February to April against May to July, which
avoids January's seasonal spike in family law enquiries:

| Location | Profile impressions | Contact actions | Action rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commerce City | 5,423 → 5,981 (+10%) | 463 → 382 (−17%) | 8.5% → 6.4% |
| Denver | 3,849 → 3,751 (−3%) | 379 → 308 (−19%) | 9.9% → 8.2% |
| Colorado Springs | 1,836 → 3,376 (+84%) | 390 → 291 (−25%) | 21.2% → 8.6% |
| Englewood | 2,261 → 2,950 (+30%) | 206 → 194 (−6%) | 9.1% → 6.6% |
| Fort Collins | 937 → 1,169 (+25%) | 181 → 148 (−18%) | 19.3% → 12.7% |
| **Colorado total** | **14,306 → 17,227 (+20%)** | **1,619 → 1,323 (−18%)** | **11.3% → 7.7%** |

"Contact actions" means calls, website clicks and direction requests from the Google
listing combined.

Impressions rose 20% while contact actions fell 18%, so the rate at which people act after
seeing a listing fell by about a third. All five locations moved the same way.

That uniformity is the informative part. Five offices in different markets are unlikely to
independently get worse at the same thing in the same quarter, so a shared cause is more
likely. The most probable one is a change in *what the extra impressions are* — Google
surfacing these listings for a wider and less specific set of searches, adding impressions
that were never likely to produce a call. Colorado Springs is the clearest case: its
impressions rose 84% while its ranking on the tracked divorce and family law searches did
not improve at all, so the extra exposure is not coming from better performance on the
terms that matter. A mid-year change in how Google counts or reports these metrics would
also fit, and cannot be ruled out from this data.

**Practically:** treat the impression growth as low-value until proven otherwise, and judge
these locations on calls and form fills. Do not read the +20% as 20% more opportunity.

## One myth worth retiring

More reviews is not the answer in Denver:

| Location | Reviews | Rating | Map footprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Denver | 224 | 4.0 | under 10% |
| Commerce City | 212 | 4.1 | 99% |
| Colorado Springs | 89 | 4.1 | 44% |
| Englewood | 50 | 4.2 | 63% |
| Fort Collins | 17 | 4.3 | 71% |

Denver has the most reviews of any location and the worst visibility. Fort Collins has 17
reviews and the second-best footprint. Within these five locations review count does not
track map visibility, so a review drive will not fix Denver — listing configuration and
competitive density are doing the work. This is five locations in one account, not a
general law, but it is enough to say a Denver review campaign is not the first move.

Ratings are a separate and legitimate concern. Every Colorado location sits between 4.0
and 4.3 stars. For family law, where prospects compare a shortlist side by side, 4.0 in
the highest-volume market works against conversion even where visibility is strong.

## What this data cannot tell you

- **It cannot confirm what caused any of the gains.** These figures show what changed and
  when. Matching those dates to the work actually performed is a separate exercise.
- **Colorado Springs' improvement is not attributable to the current engagement.** It
  happened between the 11 February and 2 March scans, at or before the point the
  engagement began, and the February baseline is a **single scan**. The same applies to
  the first part of Fort Collins' climb (21.5% to 51.3% over that same gap). The
  well-supported, during-engagement gain is Fort Collins' steady rise from 51% to 71%.
- **February through May are snapshots, not months.** February, April and May rest on one
  scan day each and March on two. Weekly scanning only began in June. Do not read
  month-to-month swings in that period as trend.
- **September is a half month**, through 14 September, and all three of the locations that
  rose did so on the same two scan days, so a common external cause is as plausible as
  three independent improvements.
- **There is no prior-year comparison.** Both data sources begin in 2026, so seasonality
  cannot be fully separated from real change.
- **August and September profile figures are incomplete.** The feed lost 5 of 31 days in
  August and 6 of 14 in September, so those months are understated and were excluded from
  the impressions and actions comparison.
- **None of this measures signed clients.** Connecting map visibility to retained matters
  requires the intake and CRM data.

## Recommended next steps, in order

1. **Confirm whether the Englewood Service Area Business conversion was intentional.** If
   not, restore the street address. Plausibly the cause of Denver's decline.
2. **Re-scan Denver and Englewood after any listing change.** If Denver's footprint
   recovers, the explanation is confirmed.
3. **Diagnose why Colorado Springs ranks worse for its own city name than for generic
   searches.** Specific, unusual, and consistent across sixteen scans.
4. **Push Fort Collins from presence to top three.** It already appears in 71% of its
   market and needs roughly four positions of improvement to start earning top-three
   share. Closest location to a breakthrough.
5. **Stop reporting impression growth as progress** until the action-rate decline is
   explained. Report calls and form fills.
6. **Address ratings, starting with Denver.** 4.0 stars in the highest-volume market is a
   conversion problem independent of visibility.
7. **Leave Commerce City alone.** At 99% footprint and position 1.8 there is no room for
   effort there to produce a return.
