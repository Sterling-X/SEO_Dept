Organic clicks, same 93-day window year over year (May 10 – Aug 10) 6,741 → 4,064 clicks. Impressions fell in step (−40.9%), yet average position improved from 38.8 to 18.3. That combination rules out a ranking penalty — the site lost the pages that were earning the clicks. 1 · The paradox — rankings up, traffic down

Monthly clicks, impressions and average position

Three separate scales, three separate plots — never one chart with two y-axes. Read them together top-to-bottom: clicks held through the autumn 2025 ranking improvement, then broke in November 2025 and never recovered.

Average position is plotted with the axis inverted so that up = better rank. Aug 2026 is a partial month (10 days).

What this rules out
This is not a ranking-algorithm penalty. A penalty pushes position down. Here position improved by 20 places while traffic halved. Improving average position while losing impressions is the signature of pages leaving the index — the deindexed URLs were the ones ranking at position 40–60, so removing them mathematically lifted the average. The clicks left with them.

Two events, not one — and this changes the root-cause hunt
A changepoint scan over the daily series (1 Feb – 10 Aug 2026, excluding the 11–12 Apr position anomaly) finds two distinct breaks, and conflating them sends the investigation to the wrong month.
  • First week of June 2026 — rankings and clicks break. Impression-weighted position steps 14.7 → 19.3 on 7 June; clicks step 52.1 → 38.2/day on 6 June. Nothing deployed in August can explain this.
  • Around 31 July 2026 — impressions collapse. There is no June break in impressions. A two-break fit on log(impressions) lands on 11 Feb and 31 July: 28,800 → 13,200 → 8,430/day. Nothing that happened in June explains this either.
  • Underneath both, a continuous ranking slide from April. On a balanced panel of the 101 Semrush slots ranked at every one of the 73 snapshots: 7.7 (Mar) → 9.3 (Apr) → 9.3 (May) → 10.3 (Jun) → 11.6 (Jul) → 13.7 (Aug).
Correction: an earlier version of this dashboard called this an “August cliff” and cited 18,757 impressions/day for May. That figure is 10–31 May only — the truncated first days of the 90-day export. Calendar May is 16,440/day, so the May→August fall is −50.1%, not −56%. April 2026 had already touched 10,056/day, so August is a relapse rather than a new floor, and May is not a safe baseline for anything — it ramped from 6.7k to 24.4k impressions/day inside the month.

7 · Conversion — the other half of the problem

Key-event rate by page type (GA4, Jan 1 – Aug 11 2026)

Session key-event rate — converting sessions ÷ sessions, taken from GA4's own rate column. Paid landing pages convert at 13.1%; the pages SEO actually ranks convert at 0.6%–2.1%.

Booking/scheduler URLs are shown at 0% because they record no key events at all — see the measurement warning below. Do not read this as a template effect. The GA4 export is a single “All Users” table with no channel dimension, so “Paid/LP” is inferred from the URL pattern, not from traffic source — the 13.1% vs 0.6% gap is a paid-vs-organic audience difference that cannot be separated from template with this file. Within the organic templates there is no reliable difference at all: city+service 4.5% vs city hubs 3.4%, Fisher p = 0.10, on 63 and 66 converting sessions across seven and a half months. Neither arm can carry a template decision. Paid also holds attention longer (118s vs 68s on location pages), so the “organic visitors are more engaged” story does not hold either.

Conversion tracking is not trustworthy
  • 55% of all key events (5,297 of 9,621) are attributed to a landing page of (not set). That bucket fires 96.3 key events per converting session against 1.41 for every other row combined — a 68× gap, which is the signature of Measurement Protocol hits or an offline import, not of user behaviour. These conversions cannot be assigned to a channel, campaign, or page.
  • 197 on-domain booking URLs took 2,266 sessions at 96.8% engagement and 177 seconds average — and recorded zero key events. Consultations booked through the scheduler are invisible in GA4.
  • Thank-you pages are counted as key events (1,638 events on 1,226 sessions), so the same conversion is counted at both the form submit and the confirmation view. Those events are tied to a page — just the wrong one. Counting them as misattributed puts the upper bound at 72.1% of key events that cannot be traced to the content that earned the conversion; 55.1% is the hard floor.
  • “Key events” is an event count, not a conversion count. Site-wide there are 9,621 key events against roughly 3,115 converting sessions — a 3.1× inflation. Every rate on this page is now computed as converting sessions ÷ sessions from GA4’s own rate column; an earlier version divided event counts by sessions and overstated every figure by about half.
Until this is fixed, no channel-level ROI claim about this account is defensible in either direction.

10 · 90 over 90 — what changed most recently

The year-over-year number and the 90-over-90 number describe two different problems Year over year the site lost impressions at flat click-through. Quarter over quarter it is losing click-through while impressions rise. Same site, opposite mechanisms — which is why a single headline number has been misleading everyone.

Search Console, three matched 90-day windows

Windows end on the last complete day (10 Aug 2026) and are exactly 90 days with an identical weekday mix, so none of this is a calendar artefact.

Read the decomposition carefully. Rising impressions added roughly 873 clicks over the quarter; the collapse in click-through rate removed about 1,314. The net is −443. Impression volume is not the current problem — conversion of impressions to clicks is. That is the same conclusion the in-market CTR test reached in section 3, arrived at from a completely different direction.

Most of the impression “growth” is one page

The civil-union post against its own 16-month average impression rate.

The page is running at 6.0× its 16-month average. That single page accounts for roughly 83% of the quarter’s entire impression rise — and it earns 68 clicks. Strip it out and the current-quarter CTR reads 0.347% rather than 0.294%. That is better, but still below the prior quarter’s 0.393%, and clicks still fell 10.2% in absolute terms. So the denominator inflation explains most of the CTR optics and almost none of the lost clicks. Both problems are real; they are just not the same problem.

Six consecutive 30-day windows

Where inside the last six months the move actually happened.

The trough is 12 Jun – 11 Jul (36.3 clicks/day, 0.263% CTR, position 19.46). The most recent 30 days are better on all three — 38.9 clicks/day, 0.319%, position 18.23. On this evidence the decline has stopped falling and is flattening, which contradicts an earlier reading in this deck that it was still deteriorating. One month is not a recovery; treat it as a floor to defend, not a turn.

Rank tracking, matched panel, first half vs second half

Semrush begins 25 March, so the longest clean split is 70 days against 70 days. Only keywords observed in both halves are counted, which removes the survivor bias that distorts a naive month-over-month average.

The volume-weighted decline is roughly twice the unweighted one (+7.03 vs +3.84 positions). The losses are concentrated in the keywords that carry the search volume — which is the single most commercially damaging shape this data could have taken.

New users specifically — and why the GA4 file cannot answer it
The GA4 export is one aggregate snapshot, 2026-01-01 … 2026-08-11, headed “All Users”.
  • No date dimension, so no new-user trend of any kind can be computed from it — not 90-over-90, not month-over-month.
  • No channel dimension, so even with dates it could not separate organic from paid, direct or LSA.
  • The New users column cannot be summed down a landing-page report. It is a user-scoped metric on a page-scoped dimension, so a visitor who lands on four pages is counted four times. The file proves it: the column totals 92,950 against 60,674 sessions — 153%, which is impossible for a real user count.
Pull Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition, primary dimension Session primary channel group, metrics sessions / new users / key events, with the date comparison set to 13 May – 10 Aug 2026 vs 12 Feb – 12 May 2026. If total new users are up while organic clicks are down 10% over the same window, the growth is coming from another channel — and that is a finding, not a contradiction.
