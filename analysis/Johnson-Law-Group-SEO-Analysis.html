2 · Why it declined — aging content vs. Google's algorithm

The most likely primary driver is a large base of old, overlapping, unrefreshed content losing favor as Google's 2024–2025 updates raised the bar on quality, originality and consolidation. This is exactly the profile those updates target — and the evidence is in the site's own data.

Ranking blog content by publish year

Top ~230 pages. Red = 2021–2022 — 55% of ranking blog URLs are 3–5 years old.

Duplication & cannibalization

The same content competing against itself — a direct core-update liability.

  • 12 duplicate URL paths — homepage indexed on both www & non-www; /reviews/, denver-divorce-lawyer and others live at two URLs each.
  • 19 overlapping truncated-slug pairs — e.g. petitioner vs respondent at 1,554 clicks on one URL and 150 on its near-duplicate twin; civil union 234 vs 59.
  • 51 pages draw 20k+ impressions each yet rank below position 20 — content Google sees but no longer trusts to rank.

The timeline fits. The site's window opens (Mar 20 2025) already depressed at ~position 39 — right as the March 2025 Core Update finished. The dramatic autumn shift (impressions halve, position 38→14, accelerating ~Sep 11) overlaps the confirmed Aug 2025 Spam Update (Aug 26–Sep 22): weak/duplicative pages lose visibility, so impressions fall while the surviving pages' average position improves. The original, larger devaluation most likely traces to the 2024 core updates (esp. March 2024, which folded the Helpful Content system into core) — but that predates this data window.

Honest caveats. This is a strong inference, not proof: Search Console gives no per-URL dated ranking history or an update label, so we're matching patterns and dates. And note the autumn-2025 improvement was algorithmic/site-side and pre-dates the March 2026 engagement — the agency inherits the cleanup, it didn't cause the earlier swing. The payoff from fixing this (consolidating duplicates, pruning/refreshing stale posts, aligning with Helpful-Content principles) should show up in clicks and new users — which is why those, not impressions, are the recovery scorecard.

4 · Why impressions & position are NOT the scorecard this year

Google changed how Search impressions are counted. Over the past year, AI Overviews and other new result surfaces began generating impressions — and shifting average position — that didn't exist a year ago. This inflates impressions and distorts year-over-year position. A rising impression count can mean "Google showed us inside an AI Overview nobody clicked," not "we're winning." The same change means Search Console clicks now under-count real organic visits — which is why GSC clicks can fall while GA4 new users rise (§2). We deliberately show no impression or position charts here to avoid a misleading trend line.

For the record, in plain numbers (context only — do not treat as success/failure):

  • Average position: the big ranking gain (≈39 → 14) landed Aug–Oct 2025, before the engagement began, then slipped ~4 positions (≈15 → 19) in Q2 2026. The slippage is a real signal worth acting on; the earlier gain was not the agency's and did not lift traffic.
  • Impressions: volatile and partly inflated by AI surfaces — not a reliable year-over-year comparison, so we don't score on it.

6 · The 90-day focus

  • Consolidate the duplicate/aging content (root cause). Canonicalize www vs non-www, merge the truncated-slug twins into one authoritative URL with 301s, and prune or refresh the 2021–2022 posts. This directly addresses what the 2024–2025 updates devalued.
  • Stop the Q2 ranking drift — diagnose the April indexation anomaly (position briefly spiked to ~63) and recover the ~4 positions lost since March.
  • Attack the money pages — Denver & Colorado-Springs divorce/custody/family-law pages from page 3–5 onto page one.
  • Grow the firm's best channel — every recovered organic click flows into the highest-engagement, highest-converting channel (§2).
  • Report on clicks + engaged sessions, not impressions. Recovery is real only when monthly clicks clear the ~1,600/mo pre-takeover run-rate for two consecutive clean months.
